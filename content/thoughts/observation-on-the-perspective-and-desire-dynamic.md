A personal structured documentation of the observation on the perspective and desire dynamic.

### 1. Prejudgement

Prejudgement is a constructor function that initializes a set of biased environmental parameters for every new informational blob the brain comes across. 

It can be simplified in wording to be heavily steered by relish. The brain manipulates itself to have prejudgemental thoughts to protect its core infrastructure of life perspective and how its dopamine cycle executes. 

The main reason is that, even though the brain has an underlying infrastructure that results in an unsustainable dopamine cycle that eventually causes corruption to itself, its main focus is always towards the dopamine. It's actually not designed to, and never initially focuses on optimization of infrastructure or anything else, because a shift in direction requires the shifting of all infrastructure since the infrastructure is built in a way that is not modular biologically throughout the evolution process. It's a massive old codebase that didn't follow the best practices back then and still needs to be maintained and relied on.

The outcome is a filtered world view that always needs enough attention and effort to pass through it, and the underlying fuels are the biases introduced by the root primitive instinct mechanism coming from the core of this infrastructure. 

In the end, there is a problem of instinct resistance that needs to be adjusted with respect to one's instinct of desire and threshold of burnout to get the most optimal yield of actualization aligned within the balance of emotional and logical purpose expected.

### 2. Getting to know oneself

From the reference point of a subject that views the inner and outer world from a biased perspective, it's important for that subject to know itself. 

The more one knows oneself, the better the parameters can be adjusted for the optimization. This allows the purpose to be settled and the inner boundaries to be seen clearly. Also, the root causes that shaped the inner environment can be identified accurately which results in the mechanism being modeled more precisely.

### 3. Instinct of desire, threshold of burnout

Instinct of desire is the backbone of the purpose seen on the surface that has primitive roots, thus substantial.

Threshold of burnout is the limit that one can handle resisting instincts.

One should have a purpose in life to have a reason to move on, and there can be many. 

Those purposes can be a result of many complex parameters of experiences, emotions, hormones, knowledge and many more. There are lots of possibilities that expand from specific niches to simple needs.

If the purpose is a goal to achieve, it needs actualization to happen which is both an optimization problem and a trade-off between now and later. 

### 4. Decision between now and later

Instincts in their purest form are driven by now, yet the actual things one wants need logic for one to take action for them to happen cumulatively. Unfortunately, logic solely has no desire. It can't drive one to action. Thus, a balance is needed.

The decision between now and later is a decision in between how much one follows their instincts, how much one resists them to satisfy logic, how much one can adjust their life to intersect both to benefit, how one should order their priorities and how much one can sacrifice temporarily or permanently for how worthwhile one of the purposes is.

The actual interesting part in this problem is that the purposes are constantly in change, and this is not in total control of logic obviously. Usually in society, suggestions tend to get closer to sacrificing now and investing in the future in a heavily summarized and exaggerated wording, but the purposes not being a strict set of constants changes the game.

The existence of the dynamic nature of the problem dictates seeking a balance in between now and later adjusted by one's own internals.

### 5. Silence brings the noise to the surface

A personal example of this problem is the social battery problem.

Purely from my experience: I have a two-sided social endurance threshold. 

The more the upper limit is exceeded, the more I feel burned out and depressed. I start hating every part of my routine, and I start being unable to stand people around me.

The more the lower limit is exceeded, the more I feel burned out and depressed yet again. I start losing self-confidence, and I start getting anxiety over life.

In the sweet spot, I experience healthy joy or boredom. When I get bored of social interactions, getting home makes me relaxed and motivates me to be more productive. When I move from home to outside, it feels great and makes me love life and admire the details.

In the end, being aware of such a social endurance threshold makes me responsible for my health to take action and control over my life for my own sake. Accepting and evaluating the dynamic nature of desire, understanding prejudgemental factors and taking enough effort to be able to analyze the problem accurately helps to maintain a balance.

Missing any part of the approach to the problem causes failed attempts and an unstable response in terms of optimality and exceeding uncontrolled thresholds, and ignoring it by not taking any action introduces some kind of amplified noise that consists of stress, anxiety, lack of purpose and mindfog. These should not be seen as corrupted products of bad actions, they are the noise that exists in the empty space of oneself that can be suppressed by taking action.

### 6. Outcome

The least one can do is be in a state of taking any kind of action. 

Then focus on oneself to get to know oneself better to optimize inner problems by considering what one really wants for a considerable future and that exact interval of life while also trying to consider if the prejudgemental perspective is causing any unwanted bias in action direction before making any adjustment towards an optimal, working subjective personal tendency, always keeping the understanding of the dynamic nature of desire between now and later.
